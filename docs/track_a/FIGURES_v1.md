## Provenance of the per-hour predictions

Figures 1 and 2 need per-hour forecasts, which the run manifests do not carry — manifests
record aggregate metrics only. `scripts/capture_predictions.py` re-ran exactly the runs
needed and recorded their identity in
`runs/track_a/predictions/per_hour_predictions_provenance.json`:

| Field | Value |
| --- | --- |
| Cell | `temperature` / `sampled` — the D-016 primary cell |
| Arm | AdaCNP |
| Seed | 20260729 (first frozen seed) |
| Context size | 64 |
| Training | 3,000 steps, stopping chosen on inner validation (D-014) |
| Folds | all 17, each re-run |
| Rows | 17,904 hours — 1,584 extreme, 16,320 normal |

Training is deterministic under the frozen seed, so these runs reproduce the sweep's models
for the same configuration.

**One model per fold, two regimes.** For each fold the *same* trained model is evaluated on
that fold's held-out event days (**extreme**) and on days from that fold's own
inner-validation block containing no event hour from any inventory event (**normal**). An
earlier plan would have compared the LOEO folds against a separately-trained
chronological-split model; that would have confounded regime with training partition. Here
model, normalizer and context pool are identical across the two curves, and only the regime
differs.

## Figure 1 — Reliability diagram

`figures/fig1_reliability.png`

**What it shows.** For each nominal probability *p*, the model's *p*-quantile is
`mean + z_p·scale`; the observed frequency is the share of outcomes at or below it. A
perfectly calibrated forecast traces the 45° line. Two curves: held-out extreme events
against normal periods, from the same models.

**What it says.**

| Regime | 90% interval actually covers |
| --- | --- |
| Normal (non-event) periods | **96%** |
| Held-out extreme events | **63%** |

In normal conditions the forecast is *slightly conservative* — its intervals are a little
too wide, which is a safe direction to be wrong in. During extreme events the same model's
90% interval covers barely three cases in five.

**The shape matters as much as the number.** The extreme curve sits **below** the diagonal
across the whole range, meaning fewer outcomes fall below each predicted quantile than the
model expects — actual load is systematically *higher* than the forecast implies. The
failure is not symmetric noise; it is a directional bias toward under-forecasting, in the
regime where under-forecasting is most costly.

**Caveats.** One seed and one arm. The two regimes have very different sample sizes (1,584
vs 16,320 hours), so the extreme curve is the noisier of the two. Normal-period days come
from each fold's inner-validation block, which is also the block used to choose the
stopping point — those days influenced *when* training stopped, though never the
parameters' fit.

## Figure 2 — February 2021 case study

`figures/fig2_february_2021.png`

**What it shows.** One day of the February 2021 event (E14_20210212): forecast mean, 90%
prediction interval, and actual ERCOT load in MW.

**Day selection is by a stated rule, not by eye:** the day of that event with the most
hours falling outside the 90% interval — **2021-02-18**, where **24 of 24 hours** fell
outside. Actual load ran roughly 10,000 MW above the forecast mean for the entire day, and
the whole observed curve sits above the upper edge of the interval.

**Caveats — read these before presenting it.**

- **This is the worst day of the worst event**, chosen deliberately by the rule above. It is
  an illustration of the failure mode, not a typical day. Figure 1 gives the average
  behaviour; this figure gives the tail.
- 2021-02-18 falls *after* the documented firm-shed window closed (`W-2021-0215-v3` ends
  2021-02-18 05:55 UTC), and the day carries **no `verified_shed` hours**. The gap shown is
  a forecasting failure, not an artifact of load shedding.
- Values are denormalized to MW using that fold's normalizer.

## Figure 3 — Coverage against event severity

`figures/fig3_coverage_vs_severity.png`

**What it shows.** Per-event empirical 90% coverage against each event's cold margin
(`margin_C` from the controlling inventory), across all 17 folds, both arms.

**What it says.** Coverage declines as events get more severe:

| Arm | Slope | r | p |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| CNP | −0.037 per °C | −0.55 | 0.021 |
| AdaCNP | −0.031 per °C | −0.51 | 0.036 |

Only the mildest events approach nominal coverage. At the severe end — E14, margin 11.9 °C
— coverage falls to roughly 0.44–0.56. **Calibration does not merely fail during extreme
events; it fails progressively harder as the event gets worse.**

**Caveats.** The trend lines and their *p*-values are **descriptive only** — they are not
part of the pre-registered analysis and no inferential claim rests on them. n = 17 points
per arm. `margin_C` is a temperature-derived severity proxy, not a measure of grid stress.

## Figure 4 — Forest plot of per-event differences

`figures/fig4_forest.png`

**What it shows.** The 17 per-event paired CNP − AdaCNP differences with 95% CIs, the
pooled mean with its CI, a line at zero, and a shaded band marking the minimum detectable
effect (±0.495).

**What it says.** The pooled effect is **+0.459** in favour of AdaCNP, with the pooled CI
excluding zero. Individual events range from +2.33 (E13) to −0.21 (E20); 11 of 17 favour
AdaCNP. The shaded band shows why no *single* event settles anything — almost every
per-event interval spans it.

**Caveats.** Per-event CIs use 2 degrees of freedom (three seeds) and are correspondingly
wide; they are shown for shape, not for per-event inference. The pooled figure is the
pre-registered quantity and is reported properly in `STAGE5_RESULTS_v1.md`. **The pooled
effect (0.459) sits just below the MDE (0.495)** — the design had roughly 77% power at the
effect it found.

## Design notes

Colours are the validated categorical palette — blue `#2a78d6`, orange `#eb6834` — checked
with the six-checks validator at `--pairs all` in light mode: CVD ΔE 24.7, normal-vision ΔE
33.6, contrast ≥ 3:1, all passing. Every series also carries a distinct marker and
linestyle, so identity never rests on colour alone.

Titles are **descriptive**; every finding appears as a subtitle computed from the data. An
earlier draft hard-coded conclusions into the titles before the stage-5 data existed, which
would have made them wrong had the data disagreed.
